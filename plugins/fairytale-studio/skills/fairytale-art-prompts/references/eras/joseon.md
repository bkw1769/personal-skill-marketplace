# Era: joseon (조선 · 전래동화풍)

Status: drafted, not yet proven by an approved sheet. First intended use: 금도끼 은도끼 (The Golden Axe and the Silver Axe), 2026-10.

## Scope

The storybook Korea of 전래동화: a Joseon-era (roughly 15th-19th century) mountain-and-village world simplified for children. Thatched cottages, stone walls, pine-covered mountains, ponds and streams, a market, the occasional tiled-roof house of a rich family or a magistrate. It is the picture-book Korea that Korean grandparents' tales live in, not a historical reconstruction: keep shapes simple and friendly and pick from the libraries below.

Fits: 금도끼 은도끼, 흥부와 놀부, 해와 달이 된 오누이, 혹부리 영감, 별주부전(토끼와 자라), 콩쥐 팥쥐, 은혜 갚은 까치, 팥죽 할멈과 호랑이, 방귀쟁이 며느리, 도깨비 방망이, 견우와 직녀.
Does not fit: Goryeo or Three Kingdoms court stories (different costume), Chinese or Japanese tales, modern Korea. Those need their own era files.

For foreign (non-Korean) audiences this module is also the main thing that makes a widely known tale look Korean, so keep the telling details (hanbok lines, jige, thatched roofs, magpie, pine) clearly visible.

## ERA BLOCK (paste verbatim)

```text
Setting: storybook Joseon-era Korea, simplified for young children. Clothing is traditional hanbok drawn with a few simple lines: commoners wear plain undyed white or pale cotton and ramie (jeogori jacket tied with a single ribbon bow, loose baji trousers bound at the ankle, long chima skirt for women), straw sandals (jipsin). Show period only through a few telling details (the jeogori tie, a straw rain hat or a black horsehair gat for gentlemen, a braided pigtail with a daenggi ribbon for children, a binyeo hairpin for married women), never through dense embroidery. Architecture, when present, is thatched-roof cottages (choga) with low mud-and-stone walls and a brushwood gate, a few tiled-roof houses (giwa) with gently curved eaves, rows of brown earthenware jars (jangdokdae), stone walls, a small pavilion, pine-covered mountains, a clear mountain pond or stream, a stone cairn by the path.
```

## Costume library

Pick one line per character and adapt the colors to the character bible.

| Role | Costume line |
|---|---|
| Woodcutter / farmer (young man) | white short jeogori with rolled sleeves and loose baji tied at the ankles, a cloth headband or a simple top-knot (sangtu) with a thin manggeon band, straw sandals, an A-frame back carrier (jige) loaded with firewood, a small iron axe held as a tool |
| Village boy | short white or pale-blue jeogori and baji, long single braid with a red daenggi ribbon, straw sandals |
| Village girl | short yellow or pink jeogori with a red ribbon tie, long red or indigo chima, braid with a daenggi ribbon |
| Village woman / mother | white or pale jeogori, long chima, hair in a low bun held by a binyeo pin, apron cloth, sometimes a water jar carried on the head on a straw ring |
| Grandmother | plain white hanbok, low bun with binyeo, slightly bent with a short walking stick, kind wrinkled smile |
| Grandfather | white hanbok, white beard, long pipe or fan, straw hat or gat |
| Rich man / greedy neighbor | rounder body, fancier hanbok in one bright color (jade, persimmon orange) with a contrasting vest (baeja), coin pouch, black gat; comic and pompous, never menacing |
| Gentleman scholar (seonbi) | long white or light-blue dopo coat, black gat with chin strings, fan or book |
| Magistrate / official | dark official robe with a square chest badge drawn as a simple patch, black samo hat with two side wings |
| King | red royal robe with a round golden dragon roundel drawn as a simple circle, ikseongwan winged cap |
| Mountain spirit (sansin / sansillyeong) | very old man with long flowing white beard and eyebrows, loose pale-grey or white robe, a long knotted wooden staff, sometimes a small cloud under his feet; serene and kind, a little playful |
| Tiger | Korean folk-painting (minhwa) tiger: round goofy face, big eyes, stripes as loose ink strokes; comic and a bit clumsy, never fierce |
| Dokkaebi (goblin) | stout cheerful goblin with one small horn, shaggy hair, simple vest, a knobby club (bangmangi) used for magic, not hitting; silly, not scary |
| Magpie (kkachi) | black-and-white magpie with a long tail, bird of good news; small and lively |
| Rabbit / turtle | simple bean-shaped animals in the style of `veg.jpg`, at most one human item (a tiny gat, a scarf) |

## Architecture and locations

| Location | Lines |
|---|---|
| Mountain village | a few thatched cottages with round straw roofs and rope nets, low stone walls, a brushwood gate, a persimmon tree, jangdokdae jars in the yard, smoke from a kitchen chimney |
| Mountain path and forest | pine trees with umbrella-shaped watery canopies, bamboo, mossy rocks, a stone cairn (doltap) with small stones, a winding path, mist drawn as bare paper between hills |
| Mountain pond | small clear pond ringed by rocks and reeds, water drawn with horizontal ink ripples and pale blue wash, a flat stone to sit on, pine branches overhead |
| Rich house | tiled roof with gently curved eaves, wooden veranda (maru), paper-screen doors, a big gate |
| Market | straw mats with baskets of vegetables and rice cakes, fabric bolts, earthenware, a wooden handcart |
| Interior | heated floor (ondol) room with a low table, sleeping mat, paper windows, oil lamp; a kitchen with an iron cauldron (gamasot) over a fire hole |

## Props

Jige (A-frame carrier), small axe, sickle, straw baskets, earthenware jars, gourd dipper (bagaji), oil lamp and hanging lantern, rice cakes (tteok) on a wooden plate, folding fan, lucky pouch (bokjumeoni), brass bowls, straw rain cape, wooden spoons and chopsticks, a spinning wheel and loom for weaving stories.

## Palette tilt

Commoners stay mostly in undyed white and pale cream with soft earth tones, so a single colored ribbon or jacket reads at a glance. Add at most two of the traditional obangsaek accents, softened to the style palette: indigo blue, persimmon orange-red, ginkgo yellow, jade green. Magical figures (mountain spirit) stay in white and pale grey with lots of bare paper around them so they seem to glow.

## Anachronism negatives (paste into Constraints)

```text
No Japanese kimono, obi, geta, samurai armor, katana, torii gates or pagoda-style Japanese roofs; no Chinese Qing-dynasty queue hairstyle, mandarin robes or red lanterns everywhere; no modern fashion hanbok with lace or short skirts; no modern Korean items (cars, power lines, plastic, sneakers, glasses, wristwatches); no European clothing; no gold or jewel-covered commoners.
```

## Audience notes

Axes and sickles are tools, never weapons. Tigers and dokkaebi are comic and soft. Mountain spirits and other magical beings appear gently: sparkles, mist and bubbles announce them before they show. No ghosts with long hair over the face, no grave scenes, no blood. For ages 3-5 see `references/audience.md`: villains look silly, figures stand out clearly from the background.

## Cultural accuracy checks

- On a jeogori the wearer's left front panel crosses over the right, and the ribbon bow (goreum) sits on the wearer's right chest.
- Married men wear a top-knot (sangtu) and often a gat; unmarried boys and girls wear a long single braid with a daenggi ribbon. A young unmarried woodcutter can wear a braid or a simple cloth headband.
- Commoners did not wear bright silk; keep rich colors for rich or royal characters.
- A Korean mountain spirit is a white-bearded old man, often with a tiger companion, not a Greek god or a Chinese emperor.
