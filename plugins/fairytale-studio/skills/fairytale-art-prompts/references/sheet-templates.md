# Sheet templates

Each template gives the composition block and caption pattern. `<...>` marks slots. Canvas: large landscape, 1536x1024 unless the tool offers more. Templates 1–3 are generalized from the approved Troy sheets; 4 and 5 are new. Template 6 (element kit) and template 8 (background plate) live in `element-kits.md`. Template 7 (character cut-out sheet) is below: it is the production source for character sprites, while sheets 1-4 on paper are for review and identity.

Rules shared by sheets 1-5, because the sheets feed an automatic cut-out step and need to read as artist model sheets:

```text
Large LANDSCAPE artist's model sheet on off-white watercolor paper, spacious, generous whitespace, no boxes, cards, borders or design-app UI. A small neat handwritten Korean title in a clear top band; no subject enters that band. Every figure is complete head-to-toe and stands apart with clear paper between it and its neighbours, even within a pair. Leave clear paper above the highest point of every figure (hair tufts, helmet plumes, ears, raised hands) and on both sides of the widest point (capes, shields, flowing hair). Each caption sits just below its figure without touching it. Exactly the captions listed, no other text. All Korean captions accurate and legible.
```

## 1. Cast lineup (anchor sheet)

Make this first. It defines identity and palette for the whole story.

```text
Asset type: first character-design concept sheet for the children's fairy tale "<title>", audience ages <age>.
Composition: <N> clearly separated groups in two rows of <N/2>, figures complete head-to-toe. Title: "<title> · 캐릭터 시안". Captions in order —
Top row: "<c1>", "<c2>", "<c3>", "<c4>".
Bottom row: "<c5>", "<c6>", "<c7>", "<c8>".
Small simple handwritten color swatches under the title if space allows.
```

Then numbered subjects `1 <c1>: <identity sentence> <pose>` for every caption. Good slot uses from Troy: one slot for a key prop, one slot `비례 비교` showing tiny main characters next to the largest prop or creature.

Keep groups to 8 or fewer; more crowds the page and shrinks faces.

## 2. Expression and pose sheet

```text
Input images: Image 1 is the approved FIRST CONCEPT lineup and is a STRICT CHARACTER IDENTITY AND PALETTE reference, not an edit target; <style refs with role sentences>.
Asset type: character expression and pose model sheet.
Composition: two rows, exactly two characters from Image 1. Title: "표정과 동작 · <name A> & <name B>".
Each row: subject caption "<name>", THREE full-body poses in order <front neutral>, <side doing X>, <3/4 doing Y>, then FOUR close-up faces: <emotion 1..4>. Exact small captions: <pose captions>, <emotion captions>.
Preserve face, haircut, clothing colors, age and distinguishing features exactly: <identity sentences A and B>.
```

Pick emotions from the story beats (Troy used 궁금/걱정/놀람/희망 and 생각/번뜩임/쉿/미소). Keep emotions child-appropriate: curious rather than scared, no crying or grief.

## 3. Prop / structure sheet

For a story-defining object or building (wooden horse, gingerbread house, tower, magic mirror).

```text
Input images: Image 1 is the first character lineup, a STRICT <PROP>, <CHARACTER> IDENTITY, PALETTE AND STYLE reference, not an edit target; <creature-4 / creature-1 role sentences>.
Asset type: <prop> prop and scale model sheet. Title: "<prop name> · 구조와 비례".
Preserve the <prop> shape from Image 1 exactly: <shape sentence>.
Composition: four airy studies, not a story scene — top left large SIDE VIEW "측면"; top right smaller three-quarter view "사선"; bottom left "<내부 구조 or 사용 장면>" <what the cutaway or use shows>; bottom right "크기 비교" with the same <prop> beside tiny same <characters> from Image 1.
No dimension numbers, no engineering diagram clutter.
```

## 4. Turnaround (production sheet)

For characters that will be animated. One character per sheet.

```text
Input images: Image 1 is the approved lineup, STRICT IDENTITY reference; Image 2 the expression sheet if one exists.
Asset type: production turnaround for <name>. Title: "<name> · 턴어라운드".
Composition: one row of FIVE full-body views at identical scale on a shared faint ground line — "정면", "3/4 앞", "측면", "3/4 뒤", "뒷면". Same height, same proportions, neutral standing pose, arms slightly away from the body so the silhouette is readable. Side view is a strict profile: nose points to the frame edge, only one eye visible. Plain paper behind, nothing between the views.
```

If cut-out puppet layers are needed later, ask for a separate sheet: `"<name> · 부위 분리"` with head, torso, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, shins, feet drawn separately with clear gaps, same scale as the turnaround.

## 5. Location sheet (mood reference only)

Mood and layout references in the same style, for the user to approve the look of each place. They are not production backgrounds: the backgrounds are pasted together from element kits (template 6, `element-kits.md`). After approval, list the elements each vignette shows; that list becomes the kit inventory.

```text
Input images: <creature-4, nature, nature-2 role sentences>.
Asset type: location concept sheet. Title: "<story> · 배경 시안".
Composition: <3 or 4> separate vignettes with bare paper around each, no frames — <location 1 "caption">, <location 2 "caption">, ... Each vignette is a wide establishing view with no characters, soft edges fading into the paper, horizon roughly at the lower third.
```

Pull location lines from the era module's architecture table.

## 6. Element kit

Separate torn-paper pieces on a flat magenta backdrop, one sheet per layer, no text. Full template, naming, layers and cutting: `element-kits.md`.

## 7. Character cut-out sheet (production sprites)

Why: on review sheets the figure sits on off-white paper, so white or pale clothing (hanbok, linen, a white cloud) has no edge against the paper and the cutter flood-fills straight into it; parts of Dori's white trousers and jacket vanished this way. On a magenta backdrop with a paper margin around each figure, the margin is what gets cut, and everything inside it survives. It also gives characters the same cut-out look as the near element pieces.

Make it from an approved pose sheet (template 2) or turnaround (template 4). One character per sheet, up to 8 figures, no text.

```text
Input images: Image 1 is the approved character lineup, a STRICT CHARACTER IDENTITY AND PALETTE reference, not an edit target; Image 2 is the approved pose sheet for <name>, the reference for each pose.
Asset type: character cut-out sheet for <name> in the children's fairy tale "<title>": production sprites to be cut out, not a review sheet.

<STYLE BLOCK from the style module>

Each figure is drawn on its own piece of off-white watercolor paper and cut out by hand around the figure, like a paper puppet from a picture book: an even off-white paper margin, about 3 percent of the figure height, follows the outline of the figure and of everything it holds, all around. The paper piece is one solid silhouette: small gaps between the legs, between an arm and the body or inside a held tool stay filled with paper. A continuous black pen outline closes every edge of the figure, and above all every edge where white or pale clothing meets the paper margin, so no part of the figure fades into the margin. Pieces lie flat, evenly lit, no cast shadow, no curl.

Composition: exactly <N> separate figures in <rows> rows, in this reading order, all at the same scale, complete head-to-toe with props inside their own paper piece —
1 <slot-name>: <pose sentence from the pose sheet>.
2 <slot-name>: ...

Preserve face, haircut, clothing colors, age and distinguishing features exactly: <identity sentence from the character bible>.

<Backdrop block from torn-paper-collage.md>

Constraints: no title, captions, labels or swatches; no ground line, floor shadow or ground patch under the feet; no effect marks (motion lines, sparkle marks, exclamation marks, sweat drops) outside the paper piece, because the video adds those; no extra characters; <style negatives>; <era anachronism negatives>; the magenta exists only as the flat backdrop.
```

Give every pose that will be used in both directions two slots, `<pose>-right` and `<pose>-left` ("same pose facing left, drawn again, not mirrored: the jacket still closes right over left"), because mirroring a hanbok or robe sprite reverses its closing (see element-kits.md, Direction and gaze). Plan facings from the story's screen geography: travel out = right, return = left, and the side of the frame where each character's targets sit.

Cut with `sheet-to-sprites` exactly like a kit: `find-pieces.mjs --names <slot names>` then `cut-sprites.mjs`. Seated poses keep their seat (rock, stump) inside the same paper piece; if the seat must be reused alone, make it a separate element piece instead.
