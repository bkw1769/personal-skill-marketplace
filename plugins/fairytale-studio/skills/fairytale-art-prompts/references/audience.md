# Audience notes for art prompts

Drawing choices by age band, from the age research of 2026-10-08 (the story-storyboard skill's `references/audience-profiles.md` holds the full profile). Grades: evidence = studies, inference = derived, not yet tested. No study gives numeric colour-saturation targets; keep the style module's palette.

## Band A: 3-5

Add to the Subjects or Constraints block:

```text
Audience ages 3-5: rounded friendly shapes, large simple eyes, strong readable silhouettes, one fixed signature colour per character, clear contrast between each figure and the background, simple uncluttered backgrounds. Villains look silly or grumpy rather than frightening: no sharp teeth, claws, blood, glowing eyes or menacing close-ups.
```

- Why: preschoolers judge by appearance and are frightened by looks and transformations even when they know it is pretend (evidence); readable silhouettes and fixed colours help them track who is who (inference).
- Transformation scenes (a witch turning into an animal) are not drawn as a sheet; the video hides them behind a transition.

## Band B: 6-8

```text
Audience ages 6-8: each character instantly recognisable by silhouette and costume colour even in a group of five; villains may look ordinary with a suspicious clue (a sly smile, a hidden tail, a too-big basket), never gory or realistic-violent.
```

- Why: casts grow to 3-5 named characters and simple deception (a disguised wolf) becomes understandable (evidence → inference).

## Band C: 9-10

- Classic villains at original-tale tension are fine; still no realistic violence. More detailed settings are acceptable.

## Shared

- Watercolor storybook art needs no AI-content label on YouTube; realistic synthetic people or scenes would.
- Each story needs its own designs; reusing one template character set across many stories looks mass-produced, which YouTube demonetizes.
