# Fairy-tale art prompts

Character sheets are generated by an image model (so far Codex built-in `image_gen`, 1536x1024 landscape). Good results came from long, structured prompts whose wording was tuned by trial; re-deriving that wording for every story drifts the style and wastes iterations. This skill keeps the tuned wording in modules and assembles prompts from them.

A prompt = **style module** (how it is drawn) + optional **finish module** (how a piece ends: torn-paper rim) + **era module** (what the world looks like) + **sheet template** (what is on the page) + **character bible** (who exactly is drawn). Styles and eras combine freely: watercolor-ink × medieval-europe or × joseon today, crayon × medieval-europe later.

**Backgrounds = far plate + near pieces** (`references/element-kits.md`): the far distance (sky, far mountains, distant hills) is one full-bleed painted **background plate** per location and time of day, with no cut rim; everything near (house, jangdokdae, stone wall, tree, pond, reeds, branch, ...) is a separate cut-paper **element piece** with a visible paper margin on a flat magenta kit sheet, cut out by `sheet-to-sprites` and pasted on the plate per scene. Never put near objects into a plate or deliver a whole-scene picture: it cannot be re-arranged, re-used or animated in layers. Location sheets are only mood references for approval.

**Character sprites come from cut-out sheets** (template 7 in `references/sheet-templates.md`): each figure on its own paper piece with a closed pen outline and paper margin, on the magenta backdrop. Review and pose sheets on paper are for approval only; cutting them loses white or pale clothing into the paper.

## Workflow

1. **Pin the brief.** Story title, audience band (`3-5`, `6-8` default, or `9-10`; see `references/audience.md` for the lines to add per band), style, era, and which sheets are needed. Ask only for what is missing and cannot be inferred; the era usually follows from the story (Grimm/Perrault tales → medieval-europe, Korean 전래동화 → joseon).
2. **Write or load the character bible.** For a new story, fill `assets/character-bible-template.md` into the story's output folder as `character-bible.md` before any prompt. Each character gets a fixed identity sentence. For an existing story, load its bible and reuse it unchanged. See `references/consistency.md` for why the sentence must be repeated verbatim.
3. **Generate in anchor order.** Cast lineup first: it becomes the identity and palette anchor that every later sheet attaches as a strict reference. Then expression/pose, turnaround, props, location mood sheets, then **character cut-out sheets** from the approved poses, the **background plates** per location and time of day, and the **element kits** for every near piece (`references/element-kits.md`; inventory them from the storyboard first and record the list in the character bible).
4. **Assemble the prompt** in this block order, pasting module blocks verbatim (paraphrasing is how styles drift):
   1. Header: `Use case: illustration-story. Asset type: <sheet> for the children's fairy tale "<title>", audience ages <age>.`
   2. Input images: one role sentence per attached image (style refs from the style module; the anchor sheet as strict identity reference when it exists).
   3. Style block (style module), then the COLLAGE BLOCK for element kits (finish module).
   4. Era block (era module) plus the costume or location lines for the characters in this sheet.
   5. Composition and exact captions (sheet template). Captions are listed exhaustively, followed by "no other text". Use Korean for review sheets; when the video is in another language and will reuse the hand-lettered title or name labels cut from the sheet (as the Troy video did), write those captions in the video's language (e.g. English for a foreign-audience channel).
   6. Subjects: identity sentences from the character bible, numbered to match the captions.
   7. Palette: style palette with the era tilt.
   8. Constraints: style negatives + era anachronism negatives + audience-safety negatives + cut-out rules. Kit sheets use the collage negatives instead of the style module's "no cut-paper collage", and repeat the backdrop block.
   Keep subject lines positive: describe what is there. Forbidden objects go only in Constraints, because naming an object inside a subject line (even "no glasses") tends to make the model draw it.
5. **Generate or hand over.** For a story with several sheets write one spec (`references/story-spec.md`) and let `scripts/assemble-prompts.mjs` paste the module blocks into every prompt and write `batch.json`; then `scripts/run-batch.sh <out dir> [sheets]` generates them with Codex. Edit the spec and re-assemble rather than editing prompt files. If an image tool is available, generate with the anchor attached; otherwise give the user the finished prompt and the list of images to attach. With Codex CLI follow `references/generation.md`: copy each image via `scripts/codex-image.mjs <log>` (the run's own image, never the newest PNG) and look at it before generating any sheet that attaches it.
6. **Record** every generated image in the story folder's `prompts.json` and README (`references/record-format.md`); kits also record `layer`, `pieces` (slot names in order) and `scale_ref`. The record is what lets anyone reproduce or extend the set without this conversation.
7. **Cut kits** right after approval with `sheet-to-sprites` (`find-pieces.mjs --names <pieces>` then `cut-sprites.mjs --rim <story paper>`), so a sheet whose pieces touch is caught while regenerating is still cheap.
8. **Review** against the checklist below before calling a sheet approved; regenerate only the failing sheet, keeping the same prompt plus a targeted fix line.

## Review checklist

- Same face, hair, outfit colors and accessory as the anchor for every repeated character.
- Style holds: wobbly pen line, transparent wash with blooms, visible paper, dot eyes. Reject glossy, vector, anime or 3D drift.
- Era holds: no anachronisms from the era module's banned list.
- Captions legible and exactly as listed; no extra text.
- Cut-out ready: figures separated with clear paper between them, clear paper above the highest point of each figure (hair, plumes, ears, raised hands), nothing touching the title band or captions, complete head-to-toe (the video pipeline flood-fills the paper around each figure, so touching groups cannot be separated).
- Element kits and character cut-out sheets: exactly the listed pieces in order, each with a paper margin all around in the same cream paper tone on every sheet of the story and a closed pen outline (white clothing included), textures drawn as irregular strokes rather than repeated symbols, no ground or shadow, flat backdrop to every edge, no text; `find-pieces` finds exactly that many pieces (full list in `references/element-kits.md`).
- Background plates: full bleed, no rim, nothing near, paler with distance; the lower part is a calm ground field in the location's own colour (earth, forest floor, waterside grass, path; never a blue wavy ground at night), and far trees are irregular dabs, not stamped shapes.
- Audience-safe: no blood, injury, frightening faces or realistic weapons; for 3-5, villains look silly rather than scary and every figure stands out clearly from the background (`references/audience.md`).

## Adding a style or era

Create `references/styles/<slug>.md` or `references/eras/<slug>.md` with these sections, mirroring the existing files:

- **Style:** reference images and role sentences, STYLE BLOCK, palette, negatives, known failure modes and fixes.
- **Era:** scope (place and period), ERA BLOCK, costume library by role, architecture and locations, props, palette tilt, anachronism negatives, audience notes.

Mark it "drafted" until a sheet generated with it is approved, then record which sheet proved it. Korean history eras (e.g. goryeo, joseon) are separate era files; "medieval" here means medieval Europe.

## Output to the user

Reply in Korean. Show the assembled prompt in an English code block (image models follow the English module wording best) with the Korean captions inside it, then list the images to attach and where the result will be recorded.
